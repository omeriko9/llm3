When the Ridgemont Public Library opened its doors in 1974, its collection filled fourteen rooms and its catalogue was a wall of wooden drawers. Fifty years later, the building looks much the same from the street, but almost everything inside it has changed. Last spring the library lent more digital titles than printed ones for the first time in its history.

The shift did not happen overnight. Staff spent three years digitising rare local newspapers, and the results were not always encouraging: early scans were unreadable, and a quarter of the collection had to be photographed twice. "We were learning as we went," said the head librarian, Miriam Alvarez. "There was no manual for any of this."

Today the library's digital archive receives more visitors each month than the reading room sees in a year. Yet the staff insist the physical space still matters. Children are read to on Saturday mornings, students crowd the study tables before examinations, and the wooden drawers, long since emptied, remain in the lobby, kept, Alvarez says, so that visitors remember how much has changed.
